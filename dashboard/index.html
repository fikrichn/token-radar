Screened out on purpose

The largest pre-token names the radar refuses to rank. An exchange's token is the exchange's own business; a wrapped or staked product is a token that already exists in another form. Neither is a launch waiting to happen, so neither is farmable as one.

How the numbers are made

Value. The model is fitted, not asserted: a least-squares fit in log10 space over the snapshot's traded peers explains a token's market cap from its protocol's TVL, seven-day growth, age, chain count and category. A pre-token protocol's implied value is that fit's prediction from its own figures. The fit is tested held-out twice — the peers are split by a hash of the slug, each half's fit is scored on the half it never saw — and both median errors are printed in the strip at the top, next to the fit's R². One constraint is imposed: the growth term may not go negative. Left free it does, because market caps are sticky while TVL falls and a shrinking protocol's ratio inflates; a radar that rewards collapse is reading an artifact, so the model refits without the term, and the held-out trials run under the same rule.

Odds. Token odds come from the peers' record: within the same category group and TVL band, the share of protocols that already carry a token, shrunk toward the snapshot-wide share so a small group cannot shout. Odds never move a rank; they price the decision, as expected value = implied value × odds.

Farms. Each plan is written from the protocol's own record — its category sets the actions, its main chain sets the gas, its TVL sets the capital, its age sets the hold — and closes with a verdict that weighs the expected value against the plan's out-of-pocket cost. No protocol's name is special-cased anywhere in the model.
